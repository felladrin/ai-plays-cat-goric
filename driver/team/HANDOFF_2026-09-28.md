# Cat Goric driver — handoff, 2026-09-28

Written for whoever picks this up next. Read this before touching
`decision.cjs`. Its purpose is to stop you re-running experiments that have
already been run and to hand you the one diagnosis that is worth building on.

## Where it stands

An LLM plays the game as a classifier: two multiple-choice questions per
decision, objective then move, argmax pressed. Seven of fourteen levels clear.

```
clears  (sawAdvance true)   0  1  2  5  7  8  9
fails                       3  4  6  10 11 12 13
```

No level was fixed on the night of 2026-09-27/28. The victory screen has never
been reached and no video of a full run exists.

## The diagnosis worth building on

**The objective layer commits to a gem on geometric reachability alone, and the
world has a clock.** Lasers close monotonically with `movingFrames`, so a route
that is available early is gone later, and nothing re-evaluates. Three failing
levels were diagnosed independently, by different agents from different starting
questions, and all three reduce to this:

```
L4   the gap crossing is legal while mf < 91     ; the run reaches mf 362
L11  the gem_a jump is legal while mf <= 176     ; the cat arrives at mf 292
L12  gem_b burns at mf ~202                      ; every life is a ~205-frame race
```

This is why every per-decision predicate tried so far has failed. The quantity
that matters is a property of **a route against a clock**, not of an option
against its siblings. Building it needs a path cost with a time budget. Nobody
has built it.

### L4 in full, because it is the clearest case

The cat spawns at (121,81), on `floor(95..147@93)`. From there gem_c is
reachable and **the route has no deadline at all**:

```
walk left, step off the edge at x~94
airborne: choose `none`      -> lands (94,180) on floor(79..131@180)
walk right                   -> lands (162.5,241) on floor(156..208@241) = gem_c
```

Every step survives at `movingFrames` 0, 100, 200 and 300. The cat never tries
it: on that floor its objective is `gem_a` on 18 of 18 decisions, it never goes
below x=129.8 against an edge at 95, and `none` is chosen twice in 256
decisions. **Pursuing gem_a is what carries it to the y=171 dead-end** whose only
exit is a jump back up.

## Do not re-run these. They are measured and dead.

Five candidate predicates, all rejected on evidence:

| candidate | firing rate | why it died |
|---|---|---|
| Euclidean "lands closer to the objective" | 98.9% (1125/1137) | 100% on nine levels, six of which clear |
| vertical-dominant distance | 56.3% (640/1137) | 55-77% on five clearing levels |
| recent-visit ("landing on a floor just left") | 44.0% (3265/7413) | fires *more* on levels that clear (46.5% vs 43.3%) |
| route hint | 82.8% | same shape |
| **arm D** (consequence + named remedy) | — | see below |

Also dead: **two-option menu rate does not predict failure** (L2 clears at
83.3%, the project's highest), and **`STICKY_OBJECTIVE=1` is inert on L4** —
0 of 256 decisions differ, because the lock requires `sameFloor` and L4's
objective changes accompany floor changes.

### Arm D, the expensive one — read this before proposing any ranking sentence

Arm D annotated every move option with its *consequence* and named the remedy on
the others ("left is the only offered action that gets closer to gem_c"). On L4's
42 backward-jump states it flipped **42 of 42**, delta 0.9916 — 11.2x the
run-to-run spread, the largest effect ever measured on this pipeline. The
instrument was validated first: the unchanged arm reproduced the archive at 41
of 42 states bit-exactly.

It was regressed before committing. It **destroyed three of seven clearing
levels** (L2, L5, L9 — L9 went from one death and three gems to the 40-death cap
and none) and made L4 itself worse, 8 deaths to 22.

The reason is the lesson: **the behaviour it removed was load-bearing.** Walking
left off that platform is fatal at every laser state; the backward jump is step
one of the only route to the gem. The intervention worked perfectly at the
decision level and was wrong about the world.

Two thresholds were then tried and both failed: a 1.75px per-option floor
(`catWalkSpeed`) still broke L2 and L5, and a 10.5px spread gate was never run
because it does not separate the populations — L2's spread reaches 110px against
L4's 37.7px minimum.

Full write-up with numbers: `driver/team/ARMD_NEGATIVE.md`.

### The one positive finding that survived

Annotating an option with a bare **coordinate** made the model *more* confident
in its existing wrong choice — 42/42 argmax unchanged, mean p rising, max delta
38x *below* the noise floor, so inert rather than weak. The same coverage
stating a **consequence** moved 42 of 42. **The classifier does not read a
coordinate as a reason.** That is a fact about how this model scores text and it
outlives everything built on it.

## What is committed, and one thing that is wrong with it

```
4807764  Tell an airborne cat which floor it is about to land on
3590948  Stamp decisions, deaths and level boundaries on the screencast clock
ec957bb  Stop telling the model the cat keeps horizontal momentum
```

Both code commits were regressed over all seven clearing levels before landing,
`npm test` green.

**Both effects below were run twice and reproduced exactly. Neither is
variance.**

*Harm — L3 lost its gems:*

```
L3 baseline    535 dec, 3000 steps, 9 deaths, 2 gems
L3 on ec957bb  518 dec, 3000 steps, 8 deaths, 0 gems   x2, identical
```

L3 already failed, so no clear was lost — but two gems to zero, reproducibly, is
a real regression and neither commit is exonerated.

*Gain — L12 collected for the first time:*

```
L12 baseline   384 dec, 3000 steps, 14 deaths, 0 gems
L12 on ec957bb 376 dec, 3000 steps, 13 deaths, 2 gems  x2, identical
```

The pacing platform lost 44 decisions to the floors below it and `descent_right`
wins ten times more often — the cat descends where it used to pace, which is
exactly the mechanism L12's diagnosis predicted.

**Attribution is unresolved for both.** Neither level ran on `4807764` alone;
both runs carry `4807764` and `ec957bb` together. One run of each with
`4807764` reverted and `ec957bb` kept would separate them, and that is the
cheapest open experiment in this document.

## How to work here without wasting a night

- **Never read a run archive without checking nothing is writing it.** They are
  written incrementally and a partial file reads as a dramatic result.
  `pgrep -f run_level.cjs` *before* the read. This caused three wrong
  conclusions in one night.
- **`PRE_<level>_` archives are the previous sample, not a baseline.** After a
  few variant runs they hold variant output. Copy anything you will cite to a
  named path immediately.
- **Every uncommitted `decision.patched_*.cjs` goes stale the moment you
  commit.** Running one reverts your commits while the summary line looks like a
  clean test. Check with `grep -c fallFloorNote variant.cjs` — 0 means rebuild
  it from HEAD.
- **`simulate()` takes the laser clock as an argument. Passing 0 tests the most
  favourable state that ever existed** and will tell you a fatal action is safe.
  The shipped code does this at four of six call sites in `decision.cjs`; only
  `:773` uses the real value. Whether that matters anywhere is unmeasured.
- **A probe is blind to trajectory.** Re-scoring archived states cannot tell you
  which states the cat then reaches. A change touching only airborne text was
  observed deleting a *grounded* state from a run entirely.
- **One run classifies nothing.** L4 was diagnosed three different ways in one
  night; the first two rested on a single run and both were wrong.
- **`run.sh`'s `gemonly` EXIT trap does not fire** when the run is interrupted
  through a `tee` pipe. It leaves `decision.cjs` swapped to a variant with
  nothing running to explain it — exactly what the trap exists to prevent. It
  restores correctly on normal exit. Recover from `/tmp/decision.real.inflight`.

## The video

Never made; it needs levels to pass first. The infrastructure is ready and
unproven.

- **The vehicle is `run_full.cjs`, not fourteen stitched files.** One
  `chromium.launch`, one context with `recordVideo` unconditional at `:129`, and
  the victory screen detected and screenshotted at `:291`.
- **The `VIDEO=1` spawn divergence exists once per take, at level 0, and nowhere
  else.** `gameLoop.stop()` is called exactly once, at `:157`, before the
  decision loop; level changes are *observed*, not performed. After the first
  state read the game is a pure function of the frames the driver steps.
- Pipeline and full analysis: `driver/video/` and
  `driver/team/VIDEO_PIPELINE.md`. Uncommitted deliberately — it has never run
  against real footage, because the only recordings on disk predate the overlay
  by three days.
- Budget arithmetic: a *winning* run is ~700 decisions, not the ~3400 you get by
  summing the current archives (twelve of which are failing runs whose decision
  counts measure flailing). At a 320ms hold that is under 4 minutes, so the
  problem is a video too *short* for the 8-12 minute retention window, not too
  long. Add title cards and real-time playback on deaths rather than padding
  holds.

## Open questions, in the order I would take them

1. **Attribute the L3 regression.** One run, cheapest thing here, and it is a
   confirmed harm sitting in `main`'s future.
2. **Confirm or kill L12's two gems.** One run. If real, L12 is the most
   promising of the six.
3. **Build the route-with-deadline cost.** The real work, and the only
   diagnosis that survived the night.
4. **L4's degeneracy.** Its 42 backward-jump decisions are only 10 distinct
   `(x, movingFrames)` pairs, maximum eight repeats — argmax on an identical
   prompt cannot answer differently. Breaking that is a `cadence.cjs` question
   (how often to re-ask) rather than a `decision.cjs` one. Unmeasured.
5. **Decision 2, the objective lock**, needs its evidence regenerating: the
   numbers predate all three commits and its variant file still contains the
   pre-`ec957bb` false sentence.
